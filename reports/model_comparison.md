> `scripts/generate_dataset.py` from the documented AdmitFlow admission-funnel
> specification. They describe two models fitted to a *simulated* funnel. They are
> **not** real-world institutional performance and must never be presented as such.

### Preprocessing (shared, fitted on the training split only)

| Stage | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Numeric | `SimpleImputer(strategy="median")` then `StandardScaler()` |
| Categorical | `SimpleImputer(strategy="most_frequent")` then `OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore")` |
| Date features | ISO-8601 parsed, converted to days since `2022-01-01` |

Because the encoder is fitted on the training split only, `cycle_id` yields 2 columns
(not 4) and `campaign_id` yields 16 (not 32); levels that appear only in validation or
test map to an all-zero block rather than creating a new column. For the Random Forest
the sparse result is densified. Both models see the identical columns; only the
estimator differs.

### Model configuration

| Model | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Logistic Regression | `LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', C=1.0, solver='lbfgs', max_iter=5000, random_state=20240817)` |
| Random Forest | `RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=500, max_depth=8, min_samples_leaf=5, max_features='sqrt', class_weight=None, bootstrap=True, oob_score=True, n_jobs=-1, random_state=20240817)` |

The forest configuration was chosen once and deliberately. `max_depth=8` and
`min_samples_leaf=5` are modest regularisers that stop small leaves from memorising;
`max_features="sqrt"` is the standard decorrelation default. `class_weight=None` is
intentional: re-balancing the classes would distort the predicted probabilities, and this
comparison is judged on log loss and calibration as much as on classification.
**No search, no cross-validation and no threshold tuning was performed for either model.**

### Neither model is universally better

The two models trade wins. On the probability-quality and ranking metrics the gaps are
small and go in both directions; the one large gap is F1, and that is a thresholding
artefact rather than a difference in ranking quality (see the next subsection). A
defensible statement about which to prefer depends entirely on what the downstream
consumer needs, and this run does not settle it:

- **Probability quality (log loss, calibration)** - the property that matters if the
  output ever feeds a scenario engine or a threshold sweep: Random Forest on validation,
  Logistic Regression on test. Note the two splits disagree here, which is itself a reason not to
  over-read either number.
- **Ranking quality (ROC-AUC, PR-AUC)**: the differences in sections 2 and 3 are small
  enough that they should not be treated as decisive.
- **Thresholded classification (F1, recall)**: the forest is much weaker *as scored at
  0.5*, almost entirely because of how it scales its probabilities. At a matched
  predicted-positive budget the two are equivalent. This is a real limitation of the
  forest configuration as fitted, not a property of tree ensembles in general.
- **Interpretability**: the logistic regression's standardised coefficients are directly
  readable and comparable in magnitude. Impurity-based forest importances are biased
  toward high-cardinality and continuous features, and a tree split value has no
  portable narrative interpretation.
- **Stability**: both models are fitted on two cycles and evaluated on later unseen
  cycles. Neither is validated beyond a single held-out cycle per split, and with
  ~3,000 rows per split these metric estimates carry real sampling noise.
- **Cost**: the forest is slower to fit and far larger to persist, and this run does not
  show a large gain for that cost. Keeping the logistic regression as the production
  model and treating the forest as a check that the linear model is not leaving much on
  the table is a reasonable position.

### Why F1 differs so much: the operating point, not the ranking

At the fixed 0.5 threshold the forest's F1 is far below the logistic regression's, and
it would be easy to misread that as the forest ranking applicants worse. It is not. The
two models scale their probabilities differently, so 0.5 lands them on different
precision/recall trade-offs.

| | Logistic Regression | Random Forest |
|---|---|---|
| Highest predicted probability, validation | 0.9665 | 0.6515 |
| Share of rows predicted positive at 0.5, validation | 23.65% | 11.48% |
| Share of rows predicted positive at 0.5, test | 23.46% | 11.56% |
| Calibration slope, validation | 0.8260 | 1.5715 |
| Calibration slope, test | 0.9153 | 1.6701 |

The forest **never predicts above 0.65**, so only about 11% of rows clear 0.5, against 24% for the logistic regression. Its calibration slope of
1.57 (well above 1.0) is the same fact stated formally: the probabilities are
compressed toward the mean. `min_samples_leaf=5` and `max_depth=8` are the cause - leaf
averages pull each prediction toward the local base rate, so the forest cannot produce
confident extremes. The logistic regression shows the opposite bias (slope 0.83,
over-dispersed). Two different miscalibrations in opposite directions.

To separate the two effects, re-score the forest at the threshold that gives it the
**same number of predicted positives** as the logistic regression at 0.5:

| Split | Model | Threshold | Predicted positive | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Val | Logistic Regression @ 0.5 | 0.50 | 711 | 0.5387 | 0.4788 | 0.5069 |
| Val | Random Forest, matched budget | 0.4229 | 711 | 0.5288 | 0.4700 | 0.4977 |
| Test | Logistic Regression @ 0.5 | 0.50 | 704 | 0.5639 | 0.5032 | 0.5318 |
| Test | Random Forest, matched budget | 0.4285 | 704 | 0.5483 | 0.4892 | 0.5171 |

Once the operating point is matched the two models are within 0.0093 F1 of each
other on validation, and their precision and recall are almost interchangeable. So the
forest is **not** the weaker ranker: at this configuration it is a similar-quality
ranker with a badly scaled probability output. Which of those two facts matters depends
entirely on how the prediction is consumed.

> **This is a diagnostic, not a recommendation.** The matched threshold above must not
> be adopted. Selecting an operating point requires a stated cost ratio - the cost of
> missing an enrol versus the cost of a wasted outreach - and the project has not
> defined one. `THRESHOLD` stays at 0.5 for both models, and choosing it properly is a
> separate, later step.

## 6. Does the advanced model introduce any leakage?

**No.** The forest consumes the identical design matrix as the baseline, produced by the
same `select_features()` call, so the leakage boundary is enforced once and covers both
models. Tree ensembles are not exempt from leakage - if anything they are *more*
exposed than a linear model, because a tree will happily split on a memorising
identifier - so three explicit checks were run.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Forbidden columns present in the feature set | **0** (allow-list intersects 28-column FORBIDDEN set) |
| Declared features used, identical to the baseline | **43** (exact match) |
| Total importance on any forbidden column | **0.000000** |
| Strongest single-feature rank AUC on the training split | **0.7226** (`doc_completeness_score`) |
| Leakage guard threshold | 0.93 - **not approached** |
| POST_OUTCOME / TARGET columns in the design matrix | **0** (enforced by `select_features`) |

The single-feature probe, measured on the training split, involves no model at all:

| Rank | Feature | Rank AUC |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `doc_completeness_score` | 0.7226 |
| 2 | `academic_score_pct` | 0.7106 |
| 3 | `annual_fee_inr` | 0.6296 |
| 4 | `demand_weight` | 0.5990 |
| 5 | `duration_years` | 0.5880 |

A leaked column would appear here as a near-perfect single-feature separator. The
strongest is 0.723, roughly 0.21 below the
0.93 guard. That is the same feature the dataset audit flagged
(`doc_completeness_score`), which scored 0.713 on the full pooled dataset against 0.93;
the small difference is because this probe runs on the training split only, not the
pooled rows. This is why `stage_reached_order` - an exact
deterministic restatement of `enrolled_flag` that is physically present in the modelling
view - is excluded by the allow-list rather than left to the model's discretion. A tree
model would find it immediately and score near-perfectly, which is a bug, not a result.

Multi-category columns are excluded from the probe above: rank-AUC on an arbitrary
ordinal encoding of a nominal level is not a meaningful leakage measure.

**Residual risks that are real but are not leakage:**

1. The forest fits 500 trees at `max_depth=8` on 6,154 rows with 124 columns,
   so it can overfit the training cycles. The out-of-bag estimate and the held-out
   cycles are the checks; the test figure is the honest one.
2. `campaign_id` is one-hot encoded and is close to a group identifier, so its
   importances mean "which campaigns differ", not a causal channel effect.
3. Rows are not independent - 2,529 applicants hold more than one application. No
   resampling was done, so the reported figures are unaffected, but any future
   bootstrap or significance test must group by `applicant_id`.

## 8. Limitations

1. **Synthetic data.** Both models are fitted to a simulated funnel whose generating
   parameters are known. This is a comparison of two estimators on known data; it says
   nothing about which would win on real institutional data.
2. **One held-out cycle per split.** Validation and test are each a single cycle, so the
   estimates carry sampling noise that a repeated or rolling-origin backtest would
   quantify. This run does not, and the two splits disagreeing on log loss is a direct
   symptom of that noise.
3. **No tuning on either side.** Neither model received a fair search. This is the
   correct first comparison, but it is not the last word: a tuned model of either family
   could close or reverse these gaps.
4. **Base rate drift.** The enrolment rate falls across cycles (0.2923 train, 0.2661
   validation, 0.2629 test), so the test split is not exchangeable with the training
   split. The test figures score better partly because the base rate is lower, which
   makes **validation the more conservative estimate**. Both models are equally
   affected.
5. **Absolute-time features do not extrapolate.** The four declared date features are
   encoded as days since a fixed anchor, so a model can learn absolute calendar position
   that a later cycle cannot confirm. This is a feature-design issue, not a model issue,
   and it penalises both models identically.
6. **Selection bias in the population.** The modelling view is filtered to
   `stage_reached_order >= 3`, which conditions on latent intent and channel. Both
   models answer "of those who submit an application, who will enrol", not "who will
   enrol among all inquiries" (the top-of-funnel rate is 0.121, not 0.279).
7. **Seat capacity is a planning attribute, not an admission cap** in this dataset. It
   is a declared feature and is used, but it must not be read as a hard constraint; in
   the generated data admits exceed declared capacity several times over.
8. **Attribution fields are associational.** Channel, source and campaign features are
   first-touch records confounded by applicant mix. No causal or uplift claim is
   licensed, and the importance table in section 5 must not be read causally.
9. **Impurity importance is a weak attribution method.** Section 5 uses mean decrease in
   Gini. A permutation importance on the validation split would be a more trustworthy
   measure and is deliberately out of scope for this step.
10. **The forest's probability scale is unusable at a 0.5 operating point.** It never
   predicts above 0.65, so its recall and F1 at the shared threshold are poor.
   That is a property of *this* configuration; a less aggressively smoothed forest would
   spread its predictions differently. Fixing it by tuning would contradict the
   no-tuning constraint of this step, and fixing it properly requires a cost objective
   the project has not defined. It is recorded here as a known defect, not addressed.

## 9. Integrity statement

> The dataset is synthetic, generated according to the documented AdmitFlow
> specification for development, experimentation, evaluation and demonstration. It does
> not represent real institutional admissions data. The metrics in this report describe
> two models fitted to that synthetic data and nothing more. Neither model is claimed
> to be universally better: they trade off probability quality, ranking quality,
> interpretability and cost on different metrics, and the held-out evidence here is one
> cycle per split.
